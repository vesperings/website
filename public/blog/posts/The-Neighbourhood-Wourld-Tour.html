I could talk about waking up at 3AM to get to Wembley, taking four buses and two hours to get there, being twelfth in line for the General Amission queue only for newcomers to completely disregard the queue and push to the front, the complete lack of organisation on behalf of the arena, the rude Israeli girls in front of us, or a whole manner of other things, but they all instantly melt away once we are in the arena.

Miraculously, we end up exactly where we would have according to our original queue position, if not only slightly further back. My view is technically great, Demonia boots meaning I can see more than the backs of people's heads, if not for the compulsion of seemingly everyone in front of me to film the entire concert with their phones held up as high as humanly possible.

I think the speakers at this venue must suck, because noise dept. sounds a litle muffled, though the crowd (from where I am standing at least) is quite receptive. I am glad, because I think his gloomy sound is nice , especially Given and Somebody, and he just generally seems like a sweet guy.

After a significant break to get set up (during which I sit to conserve my energy), Nessa Barrett follows. I had no knowledge of her beyond being a TikToker, so I am pleasantly surprised to discover she can actually sing. Not really my style, but not offensive either. Her music is very reminiscent of Lana del Rey, and goodness I had no idea she was so popular because the crowd goes WILD, and a significant number of people leave when she finishes her set. Lucky me, because it means I can inch closer to the stage. I also must point out how stunning the blue dress she performed in was and I want it I want it I want it.

Another break, some more sitting (my poor feet are so very grateful), and then finally... The Neighbourhood. God. I have never felt so free and alive. I can see Jesse perfectly well and sing and dance along the entire time. I joke that it is my concert as much as it is theirs. I do not take a single photo or video, which is a great idea in the moment, but I retrospectively regret it a tiny bit. No matter, because my experience belongs solely to me this way.

I had made a sign the other day, just words typed onto a word document in 80px font and printed out, and hold it up from time to time during the show. I think I catch Zach Abels notice it, and later, during Softcore of all songs, Jesse points at it!

The band does not interact with the crowd much during the show, and Jesse's voice seems slightly strained. Not bad at all, but I do wonder if he is ill. Nevertheless it is still an amazing performance and I have so much fun. I have no complaints, other than it being over far too soon (and not a single song from the mixtape being performed, but that's a pipe dream anyway.)
