Topsy Turvy (S1E10)

★ ★ ★ ½

Bennett and Yin are shocked to find Joker has escaped Arkham Asylum and is seeking to kidnap everyone who brought him down, intending each victim to be encased and paralyzed in large playing-card styled boxes. Batman investigates, but soon discovers Joker has involved an accomplice in his schemes, prompting him to confront the real criminal. Joker soon proves to be far more devious, escaping for real and leaving Batman to save his intended victims from a tragic fate.

The backgrounds in this show are gorgeous. Yin and Bennett's Batman chasing is becoming repetitive, and I hope there is some progression or development in that area. Joker is very creepy and chaotic, but so far nothing makes him stand out as THE archnemesis, since he feels equal to the other villains in terms of the challenge he poses to Batman.

The Big Dummy (S1E9)

★ ★ ★ ½

Alfred attempts to coax Bruce into engaging in online dating, hoping he may find solace with a loving companion, but Batman is required to investigate robberies conducted by Arnold Wesker, a ventriloquist with a split personality that leads the worst out through his ventriloquist dummy Scarface. While Bruce attempts to engage in Alfred's efforts, he questions whether he can, especially when he learns Wesker's robberies are the basis for a much bigger heist.

Batman online dating is an absurd concept, but it works to show Bruce's conflict between his two lives. A very paternal and caring Alfred wants a normal life for him, knowing too well that is the one thing he cannot have. The character work is good, but the Scarface plot is weak. Robot Scarface defeats the point in my opinion, and he does not look enough like a vintage gangster.

Q&A (S1E8)

★ ★ ★ ½

Batman investigates the abduction of Ross Daren and Herbert Ziegler from separate public events that occurred within the same day, shortly after each victim was publicly humiliated. He realizes the culprit is Arthur Brown, an overweight manchild and former quiz-show whiz kid who appeared on Think, Thank, Thunk, which both victims were hosting. Brown, now calling himself Cluemaster, abducted Yelena Klimanov, his childhood opponent who defeated him on the show, and Batman races to save all three victims.

Interesting parallels between Cluemaster, who never grew up, and Batman, who never had a childhood, both poisoned by vengeance. It wraps up nicely with Bruce and Alfred sitting down to play checkers together. This version of him is so human, and though he still feels different to the Batman I am used to, I really like him.

The Big Heat (S1E7)

★ ★ ★ ½

Bruce is concerned when Mayor Marion Grange informs him that a contract for work on the children's hospital may be awarded to GothCorp, a company with a dubious reputation, rather than Wayne Industries despite his father's charity work. At the same time, Batman confronts Firefly while investigating break-ins at tech firms around the city. When his effort to sway the city council fails, Bruce becomes suspicious of GothCorp's bid and finds a connection between it, Firefly and the break-ins.

I enjoyed seeing an episode dealing with Batman struggling to balance his responsibilities as Bruce Wayne and the Dark Knight, as well as him learning that he can use elements of both his lives to enrich each other. Firefly's design was cool.

The Big Chill (S1E6)

★ ★ ★ ½

Batman investigates a recent jewellery robbery from a yacht in Gotham Bay and discovers it to be the work of Mr. Freeze. To his horror, Batman discovers Freeze was once an ordinary thief named Victor Fries, whom Batman had thought dead after pursuing him over stolen diamonds until a sequence of events caused him to crash into a cryogenics lab and suffer a freak accident. Knowing he must stop him, Batman is forced to find a way to combat his new powers and confront his actions.

While Mr. Freeze's design is very cool (haha), I do prefer the more tragic take on his character over being a low level thief. I also really liked Batman's white suit. The standout element was Bruce's guilt about both being a criminal himself and being the cause of the super-villains emerging in Gotham. It is true, in a sense, because for criminals to continue effectively with Batman in town, they must increase their efforts to have a chance against him, as seen with Bane. But equally, had Batman done nothing, the crimes would continue regardless. It is Fries' own actions that lead him into the cryogenic pod, and he might have ended up in the same position regardless had it been the police chasing him.

The Man Who Would Be Bat (S1E5)

★ ★ ★

At Wayne Industries, Bruce becomes suspicious when Dr. Kirk Langstrom's work draws the attention of Yin and Bennett while pursuing his alter ego. Although Langstrom claims he is working on a cure for deafness, Bruce discovers he is working on a mutagenic serum that allows him to transform into a giant beast dubbed "Man-Bat". Batman must put a stop to him when he learns that Langstrom must feed on blood and may soon attack humans.

Ridiculous that the GCPD would think such an old man could be Batman, but he did turn out to be Man-Bat, so perhaps they are not completely misguided. Bruce knowing sign language is very sweet; it subverts what is expected of an arrogant billionaire and shows the heart he has. The episode begins with a lot of tension as it seems GCPD will investigate Bruce in relation to Batman, which does not end up being the case, but I hope the series goes there, especially as Bruce is a good friend of Bennett.

The Cat and the Bat (S1E4)

★ ★ ★ ½

Batman encounters a new foe in the form of skilled thief Selina Kyle, who operates under the alias of Catwoman. After thwarting her attempt to steal a figurine from its mysterious owner, Batman loses his utility belt to her. Not only does he face problems when she messes with it, he discovers she might use his gadgets to make a second attempt on the figurine, and she may have underestimated its owner.

This episode was so much fun. Batman and Catwoman's physical chemistry while they fight the dragons is really satisfying. I kept expecting her to betray Batman to save her own skin, but they had each other's back throughout. Her design as both Catwoman and Selina are great too. I really hope there is more of her, and that her and Batman's civilian identities interact.

Traction (S1E3)

★ ★ ★ ½

Seeking revenge on Batman for ruining their criminal operations, three mob bosses meet and hire a mysterious villain named Bane to finish him off once and for all. Batman quickly discovers that Bane is no ordinary criminal, as he can gain superhuman strength through venom pumped into his body through a unique infusion system. Badly injured confronting him, Bruce is forced to find a means to put a stop to Bane as he begins to terrorise the city.

Bane is one of my least favourite members of Batman's rogues gallery, and this episode does nothing to change that. He looks a little like Four Arms from Ben 10. I really like the way Alfred and Bruce's relationship is depicted here, between Absolute Batman and Caped Crusader, this is a welcome return to form, and the mutual love between them is clear. Batman just keeps getting eviscerated in this show by every villain, which I quite enjoy.

Call of the Cobblepot (S1E2)

★ ★ ★

A spate of thefts involving vultures, owls and crows that steal jewels leaves Batman determined to discover the culprit. At the same time, Bruce finds his latest party at Wayne Manor gatecrashed by slobbish socialite Oswald Cobblepot, whose family Alfred Pennyworth's grandfather worked for. Bruce soon realizes Oswald, going under the name the Penguin, is responsible for the thefts, and has to stop him when Alfred is captured trying to retrieve a serving tray he stole.

The Kabuki twins are so cool, almost spider-like, and have the potential to be very creepy. I was surprised that Penguin was such an even match for Batman. The fight scene in particular felt very much like an animated comic book. Alfred's history with the Cobblepots is also interesting, giving him his own motivations outside of Batman. This version of Batman so far seems to lack that certain gravitas I am used to, though perhaps it is a symptom of him being relatively early in his career. I did like that moment of him smirking at Ellen as Bruce Wayne after recognising her as the one who pointed a gun at him in the previous episode.

The Bat in the Belfry (S1E1)

★ ★ ★

Masked vigilante The Batman, alias billionaire industrialist Bruce Wayne, is publicly acknowledged as a menace by Chief of Police Angel Rojas despite his work to combat crime over the past three years, who assigns Detective Ethan Bennett and his new partner Ellen Yin to capture him. At the same time, a break-out of inmates at Arkham Asylum leads Batman to discovering that the culprit is a crazed clown criminal calling himself The Joker, who has a gas that causes victims to be left paralyzed and have a fixed, yellow-toothed smile. Batman quickly discovers that The Joker plans to use the gas on Gotham City and attempts to stop him, with Yin and Bennett eager to do the same.

A little overtly exposition heavy, but that is to be expected from a pilot. It is a bold choice to start the series with the Joker, who looks rather like some sort of Creepypasta character. I am conflicted about the animation style; the backgrounds are beautiful and almost surreal, which makes Gotham feel like its own place and has a comic book quality, but something about the way the characters are drawn clashes with it almost jarringly. It is too early to judge, but as of now, Batman's character feels very different, aggressively like a 2000s-2010s teen boy Cartoon Network show protagonist. He eats boxed cereal and nachos, for one. I am not sold yet, but do not dislike it either. Batman releasing the Joker gas into the water immediately made me think of Batman: The Animated Series' laughing fish, and it cannot have been a coincidence.
